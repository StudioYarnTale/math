Questions & Answers

Who is Math Insight for?

Math Insight is intended for senior secondary school students, school leavers and university applicants. Whether you are preparing for GCSE, A-Level, European school programmes, SAT®, ACT®, AP®, entrance examinations, scholarship tests, interviews or mathematical Olympiads, lessons are adapted to your individual goals, current level of knowledge and future plans.

Are lessons individual or in groups?

For secondary school students, lessons are usually individual. Every student has different goals, different strengths and different gaps in knowledge. A personalised approach allows lessons to be adapted to each learner's needs and pace.

How do I book a lesson?

Simply get in touch using the contact form or by email. We will discuss your goals, current level of knowledge and choose a suitable learning plan together.

What language are the lessons taught in?

Lessons are primarily taught in Russian. When preparing for studies or examinations in English-speaking countries, English mathematical terminology can be introduced naturally during the lessons.

Will a few lessons solve all the problems?

Probably not. Mathematics requires time, regular practice and personal effort. There are no magic shortcuts.

Can you make my child enjoy mathematics?

No one can make another person learn. Real progress begins when a student is willing to think, ask questions, make mistakes and keep trying. A teacher can explain, encourage and support, but genuine learning always depends on the student's own effort.

Do students simply attend the lessons?

No. Passive attendance alone rarely leads to success. Learning mathematics means thinking, solving problems, asking questions and practising between lessons. Lessons provide guidance; real understanding develops through active participation.

What if my child has gaps in knowledge?

That is perfectly normal. We first identify the missing pieces and then build understanding step by step. A strong foundation is much more valuable than rushing through the syllabus.

How often should students practise?

Regular practice is far more effective than occasional intensive study. Even twenty or thirty minutes of thoughtful work several times a week can make a significant difference.

Who will benefit most from Math Insight?

For motivated secondary school students who want to understand mathematics, prepare for examinations, improve their confidence and develop independent mathematical thinking.

What is our goal?

Our goal is not simply to improve examination results. We aim to help students become confident, independent thinkers who understand mathematics and know how to apply it in new situations.
