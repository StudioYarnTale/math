Mathematics is much more than numbers and formulas. It is a way of thinking, exploring, asking questions, and discovering patterns. In the Math Playground, mathematical ideas are introduced through stories, fairy tales, everyday situations, games, and creative activities. Children are invited to solve problems because they are curious about what happens next, not because they are required to complete another worksheet.

Learning Through Stories

Every topic is presented as part of a larger story. Friendly characters from the world of YarnTale appear throughout the lessons, facing real-life situations where mathematical thinking naturally becomes useful. Instead of abstract exercises, children help solve problems, make decisions, discover patterns, compare, measure, estimate, count, and experiment. The stories are connected into one shared world, making every new activity feel like the next chapter of an adventure.

Interactive Learning

Each topic includes interactive activities where children can explore ideas, test mathematical rules, and experiment safely. There is no penalty for mistakes. Children may repeat activities as many times as they wish, complete only part of a task, or simply explore different solutions at their own pace. Learning becomes an investigation rather than a test.

Every Child Learns at Their Own Pace

There is no rigid curriculum and no fixed timetable. Activities are organised from simple to more challenging, allowing every child to move forward comfortably. Children are free to skip activities, return to them later, or spend more time exploring the topics they enjoy most.

Learning in Small Groups

Most sessions take place in small groups of around five children with similar levels of experience. This creates a friendly atmosphere where children can discuss ideas, play mathematical games, exchange thoughts and solve problems together. The teacher carefully guides each session, making sure every child is involved and nobody is left behind.

More Than Mathematics

Mathematics is part of everyday life and naturally connects with many other subjects. Lessons often include nature, language, drawing, crafts, art, simple experiments, conversations and observations. Children gradually discover that mathematical thinking appears everywhere around them.

Curiosity Comes First

The goal is not to memorise formulas as quickly as possible. Much more important is helping children become curious, confident learners who enjoy asking questions, exploring ideas and gradually discovering that mathematics is a natural part of the world around them.



Questions & Answers

If you cannot find the answer to your question, please feel free to write to me. I will be happy to answer personally. The most interesting and frequently asked questions will be added to this page.

What language are the lessons taught in?

The lessons are conducted mainly in Russian. In the future, English-speaking groups may also be available. Important mathematical terms are explained in Russian, English and German, helping children connect new ideas with the terminology they already know from school.

What age are the classes suitable for?

The Math Playground is designed mainly for children aged 7–11. Groups are formed according to the children's current level rather than strictly by age, allowing everyone to learn comfortably and enjoy working together.

Are homework assignments compulsory?

No. Homework is always optional. Children may complete all tasks, only part of them, or simply continue with the activities they enjoy most. Parents do not need to supervise or help unless the child asks for support.

What happens during the lessons?

Mathematics is introduced through stories, games, puzzles and practical activities. Children also draw, make paper models, solve geometry challenges, build simple constructions, talk, explore ideas and work together. Many activities are connected with everyday life, nature and creativity.

What equipment will my child need?

A notebook, pencils, coloured pencils, ruler, compass, protractor, set square, drawing paper or card, scissors and glue are sufficient for most activities. Some projects may also use a simple caliper. For online lessons, a computer with headphones and a microphone is recommended.

May children use AI tools?

Yes. Artificial intelligence can be a useful learning assistant. Children are encouraged to think independently first, then use AI to explore ideas, check their reasoning or ask additional questions rather than simply copying answers.

Does my child need to enjoy mathematics before joining?

Not at all. Many children join simply because they enjoy stories, puzzles, games or making things with their hands. Curiosity often comes first, while an interest in mathematics develops naturally over time.
